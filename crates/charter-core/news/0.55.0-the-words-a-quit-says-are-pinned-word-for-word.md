Housekeeping with one behavioural finding in it, from #810 — the deletion-sweep survivors
#796 named and did not close.

## Sixteen sentences that could be reworded in silence

Every assertion the quit/close/reopen suite made about wording went through the module
constant on **both** sides:

```python
self.assertEqual(rows[0].title, leave.NOTHING_OPEN)
self.assertIn(leave.RESUMES, out)
self.assertEqual(said.call_args[0][1], commands_frame.NO_TRANSCRIPT)
```

so rewording the constant moved the test with it and stayed green. It is the same rule that
closed the on-disk names last release — **a round trip cannot pin a name** — with a sentence
in place of a file name, and the same remedy: spell the words a second time, by hand, at the
surface they reach. The duplication *is* the assertion.

Sixteen constants are now written out in the words an operator reads: the four resume
sentences, the two doorway rows, the "nothing open" row, the close row's refusal, `charter
reopen`'s "nothing recorded", both transcript refusals and the missing-pager line. Every one
was confirmed to redden when the constant is re-spelled.

## Three of them were filed as something they are not

#810 singles out `leave.OPEN_ID`, `GO_ID` and `CHAT_ID` as *"palette action ids — a binding
surface a `charter.toml` can name, so a rename silently breaks an operator's keybinding"*,
and calls them the highest-value three in the group.

**They are not action ids and no config can name one.** Measured: `component.usable_id(
"leave:quit")` is `False` — the `:` is deliberate and `frame/leave.py`'s own comment says
why — `palette.matches` and `palette.exact` both gate id-matching on `usable_id`, and
`_draw_palette` routes them before `ActionRegistry.invoke` precisely because handing
`leave:quit:go` to it would answer "no such action". They are minted and read back inside
one process and never drawn, written to tmux, or typed. The only config-named binding
surface in the frame is a component's `key`, which names a component.

They are pinned anyway, for the smaller true reason: the three shapes have to stay tellable
apart, because `verb_of` distinguishes a doorway from a per-chat row by shape alone, and
`usable_id` has to keep refusing all three or a provider's action could collide with one.

## And two sentences had already drifted

`commands_frame.NO_TRANSCRIPT` and `builtin_actions.NO_TRANSCRIPT` are filed in #810 as *the
same sentence in two places*. They are not, any more: the palette row's carries the
``(`F2 → charter: quit`)`` route and the command's does not. Both spellings are now pinned
and the difference is asserted, so making them one sentence is a change somebody makes on
purpose rather than a drift nobody sees.

## A committed value charter prints back cannot own the line

The reopen path repeats a manifest's `harness` and `cwd` back at the operator, and
`charter frame-close` repeats the chat name it was given. Every one of those values was
already one line of printable ASCII in every test in the suite, so the `contain` wrappers
around them were the identity function on every input and nothing could tell whether they
were there.

They are now driven with a value carrying `ESC [ 2 J` — erase the display — and a newline,
off the manifest, which is a plain file that outlives the process and can be older than the
charter reading it. The line stays one line and the escape is shown rather than executed.

The chat-name wrapper turned out to have a second job the sweep's own note did not mention:
`chats.ID_RE` is `[A-Za-z0-9._-]+` with **no length bound**, so a four-thousand-character
chat id passes the shape check and reaches the "no open chat" refusal intact. That case is
pinned as the budget property it is — ten times the input, the same length of line — rather
than against a fixed number.

## One guard that no capture in the suite could reach

`_capture_transcript` encodes with `errors="replace"`, and `str.encode` looks a handler up
**lazily** — only when an error actually occurs. Every capture the suite supplies is
encodable, so the name was never read and no test could tell the handler from any other
string. It is now driven with a capture that cannot be encoded, through the same seam the
rest of that class uses, and the class's own claim — *the capture never raises* — covers it.
Said plainly in the test: today's read path decodes strictly, so a pane charter cannot decode
raises upstream rather than arriving there. The handler is a floor for the day that changes.

Nothing to adopt.
