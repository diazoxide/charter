A MEASUREMENT WRITTEN AS A STANDING FACT IS A VACUOUS ASSERTION WITH A LONGER FUSE. Stated 2026-08-31 by the #710 author while correcting two of its own published numbers: 'A number stated as a live fact in a file nobody re-measures is the same defect as a test that passes on both states; it just takes longer to go wrong.' Both its numbers had drifted between measurement and merge — the escape-spelling differential 24 -> 28 (it grew while the author closed the sweep's own gaps), and the commit-message density 25/30 -> 26/30 with the command-shaped subset 10 -> 19. Neither drift was caused by anything failing; both were caught by RE-MEASURING before merge. The fix is not just updating the number: PHRASE IT AS A MEASUREMENT THAT WAS TAKEN, not as a property that holds — 'measured on N at commit X' rather than 'N of the last 30'. Then state separately the claim the number supports (here: 'that surface is dense with backticks'), because THAT is what has to stay true, and it survives the drift the raw count does not. Applies to issue bodies, docstrings, ADRs and news entries — anywhere prose carries a count nobody will re-run. Same family as [[an-assertion-that-searches-for-a-token-the-chang]]: the failure is silent and the artifact keeps looking correct.
