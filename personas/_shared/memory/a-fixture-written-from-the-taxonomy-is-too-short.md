A FIXTURE WRITTEN FROM THE TAXONOMY IS TOO SHORT TO CATCH THE BUG; ONLY THE REAL TREE IS. Stated by the #754 author (2026-08-31) after its own fix re-introduced the collision it was fixing: 'The fixture could not have found it; it was too short, and it was too short because I wrote it from the shapes rather than from the tree.' Its reasoning had been sound AND wrong — siblings differ in 'after' by construction, so a discriminator drawn from 'after' looked PROVABLY sufficient, and it is, right up until you truncate it. What caught it was re-running the same tree-wide count that produced the BEFORE numbers (11,338 mutations), which cost about two minutes and REQUIRED NO SUSPICION. Operational rule, and the cheap half is the point: when you fix a class of defect, the measurement that found the class is also the test that your fix worked — re-run it over the fixed tree before you believe a hand-built fixture. A fixture enumerates the shapes you thought of; the tree contains the ones you did not. Related: [[a-fix-for-a-class-of-bug-is-unusually-likely-to--2]].
