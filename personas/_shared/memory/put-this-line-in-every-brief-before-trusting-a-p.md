PUT THIS LINE IN EVERY BRIEF: "before trusting a passing test near your change, verify it fails without the guard." Proposed 2026-08-31 by the #783 author as the single instruction that would have got them to their two best findings faster than reasoning from the code. It is the operational antidote to the whole vacuous-assertion class this session kept hitting — seven instances across five branches, none of which ever went red. Their two examples are the shape: (1) a loose-file test in chats.py was GREEN EITHER WAY until they planted a session pointer, so it had stopped observing the case it was named for; (2) git branch --merged reported 28 squash-merged branches as NOT merged, a guard answering confidently about the wrong question. Both are the same failure: an assertion that still runs, still passes, and no longer sees its subject. The counter-measure cannot be "run the tests" — it has to be a deliberate red: delete the guard, confirm the test dies, restore. Corollary for coordinators, which was MY failure four times tonight: a brief must also say WHO MERGES. Four agents spent report cycles flagging a merge under the owner account as a possible compromise, because my briefs said "do not merge" and never said "report and I will verify and merge on your word".
