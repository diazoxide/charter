THE DELETE-THE-EQUIVALENT-MUTANT RULE NEEDS A MEASUREMENT, NOT A READING — a line that looks exactly like three dead ones can be the load-bearing one. Measured 2026-08-29 in tools/sweep.py: findings 10, 12 and 13 were all deleted as provably unreachable (merge's is_dir() — Path.glob already yields nothing on a missing path; parse_shard's 'not sep' conjunct — never the deciding clause; expected_shards' .strip() and its AttributeError catch — int() already strips and str() cannot raise it). Finding 14 sat beside them, looked identical in shape, and was the OPPOSITE: expected_shards' str() is what stops '3.9' being read as three shards. Deleting all four on inspection would have shipped a real defect. So: the rule 'an equivalent mutant and dead code are the same finding, delete the line' is correct, but 'equivalent' must be established by running the mutation and observing no difference — never by the line looking redundant.
