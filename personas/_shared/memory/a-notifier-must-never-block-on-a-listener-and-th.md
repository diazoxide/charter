A NOTIFIER MUST NEVER BLOCK ON A LISTENER — AND THE MATURE ANSWER IS RESYNCHRONISE FROM TRUTH, NOT JUST POLL. charter rejected a FIFO three times because opening one for write blocks until a reader exists, and chose 5 Hz polling because it cannot hang. Primary-source survey of ten systems (docs/research/2026-09-05-interface-agnostic-cores.md): all eight mature ones refuse to let a listener slow the writer, and every one of them degrades by telling the client to re-read truth rather than by buffering — inotify IN_Q_OVERFLOW, FSEvents "you must do a full scan, there is no way to determine what may have changed", Watchman is_fresh_instance, Kubernetes 410 Gone, tmux %pause. Kubernetes says it in a code comment: "Since we dont want to block on it infinitely, we simply terminate it", with a metric counting it. tmux answers charters literal case in C: `if (attached_clients == 0) off = 0;` — zero listeners means keep reading, throttle only when listeners EXIST and are all behind. The young protocols have not solved it: ACPs spec and 246KB schema say nothing about a dead peer and its SDKs buffer gigabytes in seconds; MCP in 2026 tells clients to "rely on polling to preserve freshness of results". So polling was the right call, and the missing half is a documented resync-from-truth path for a listener that fell behind.
