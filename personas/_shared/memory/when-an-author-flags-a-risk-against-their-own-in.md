WHEN AN AUTHOR FLAGS A RISK AGAINST THEIR OWN INTEREST, MEASURE IT — DO NOT JUST NOTE IT. Measured 2026-08-31. The #714 author disclosed that their fix added a list-panes call which "shifts every panel start ~5 ms later relative to the gather bump, a nudge on the wrong side of a 1-in-6 race" (#748). I relayed that warning to the next agent and treated it as a caveat. It was worse than the disclosure: alternated run-for-run on one machine, one commit apart, 69b59bf gave 0/20 duplications and d5f4896 (#714, merged that night) gave 13/20 — 65 percent. The fix did not NUDGE the race, IT CREATED IT. Two further facts from the same investigation, both of which invalidate the casual approach: the rate moves about 3x with machine load (18 percent idle, 64 percent loaded), so any batched before/after comparison is invalid and runs must be ALTERNATED; and the decisive evidence was the pane PAINT HISTORY (on main the top pane painted exactly once per launch, 25 of 25), not the rate — because a rate distinguishes "less likely" from "more likely" while the paint history distinguishes "we removed the state it lands in" from "we made it rarer". RULE: a self-flagged regression risk is the cheapest lead anyone will ever hand you; spend the hour and measure it before merging, because the person best placed to see it has already told you where to look.
